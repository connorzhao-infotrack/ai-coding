The flow

End to end — what the client sees

The agent moves the client on by itself wherever there is only one sensible next step. Prompts appear only at a genuine choice, or where something is still owed. At most three prompts show at once — when more qualify, the lowest-priority ones drop silently.

Please confirm or correct

  1. Booking status yields to the financial three under the cap. Booking status and the financial cycle overlap — the invitation can still be open when adjustments land — so four prompts qualify and only three fit. The financial work is what the client is being asked to act on, so booking status drops. Lexi flagged this drop order as needing her decision; this is the recommendation, not her answer.
  2. "Upload more documents" is unconditional after the documents step. Her written flow gated it on missing documents still being detected; on the call she framed it as a standing reminder that a milestone is not one-off. It is built as the latter — no detection service needed, but the prompt is long-lived (until settlement).

Two smaller wording changes also went in and are easy to revert: labels are now sentence case throughout (so "Check settlement booking status", not "Check Settlement booking status"), and the directions prompt reads "View direction to pay" singular, matching the design frame rather than her written plural.

Decisions behind the flow

DecisionSource
1No LLM in the next-step path — every decision is a fixed rule over order state.Connor
2Prompts are state-derived and survive refresh — not tied to the last exchange. "It is all about what makes sense at that stage of the matter progression."Lexi · 27 Jul
3Onboarding hands off at task creation, not consumer completion; only an owed manual upload holds it back.Connor + Lexi
4Manual onboarding documents are five types — VOI, client authorisation, purchaser declaration, mortgage discharge authority, trust account authority. "Manual" = the consumer opted out.code · confirmed
5Booking status follows the invitation, not the financial cycle. "Invitation and settlement adjustments have no correlation — some bank may join one hour before settlement."Lexi · 27 Jul
6Maximum three prompts at once; lowest priority drops silently.Lexi · 27 Jul
7One approval finalises everything — adjustments, directions to pay, trust and registry documents. "You can't approve one without the other."Lexi · 27 Jul
8Settled matters show nothing.Lexi · 27 Jul
9Purchase and Sale behave identically; non-LEAP clients get the same flow, only without the automatic document pre-load.Lexi · 27 Jul
10Prompts only need to cover the popular case. "We should stop thinking about the edge cases." Anything else is reachable from the milestone list.Lexi · 27 Jul
11Prompts render in the composer's controls row, inline after the attachment button — this supersedes the earlier "docked above the input" decision.Connor · 28 Jul
12Overarching status ("what's the progress?", "what else do I need to do?") → separate ticket, needs AI. Settlement funds summary → out until Jerol's task lands.Lexi + Connor

⚠ External dependency — the "active action" lock

Lexi raised that while an action is live in the chat (the documents card awaiting confirm), the client arguably should not be able to start something else — "grey out all the pills, and grey out the chat area until they click the buttons" — unless they cancel and abandon the task. That cancel/abandon concept does not exist yet. This flow assumes the current behaviour: users can jump out at any time, and the follow-up prompts are what bring them back. If the lock is built later it sits above next-step, not inside it.
