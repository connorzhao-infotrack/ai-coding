DTMF Reliability — Sydney Water IVR

The AI presses the right digits, the IVR receives something, and answers "the property number you have entered is invalid". A human on a phone always succeeds. This page records what we know, what we ruled out, where the fix stands, and the exact experiments still to run.

1 · The symptom

CallerAuthorityResult
Human on a real phoneSydney Waterworks, every time
AI via Telnyx send_dtmfBlacktown Councilworks
AI via Telnyx send_dtmfSydney Watermostly fails — "invalid" or "not able to record"

Two distinct failure messages appear in the transcripts, and they mean different things:

  • "The property number you have entered is invalid" — the IVR collected an entry, but the digits were wrong (corrupted/merged/doubled in transit, or a split entry).
  • "We have not been able to record your property number" — the IVR collected nothing or too little.

Both appear across attempts, so digits are sometimes corrupted and sometimes (partially) lost.

The pivotal realisation

The first in-band attempt (#121) was judged "not working" while the outbound stream encoder was hard-coded to µ-law. On calls where Telnyx negotiated PCMA, every synthesized tone decoded as noise at the far end. That bug was fixed for AI speech in #124 — after the revert — meaning the in-band approach was never fairly tested. Since in-band tones are exactly what a human keypad produces (the one thing that works 100% at Sydney Water), it earned a properly-engineered second attempt.

4 · Where we are now

PR #131 (merged) reinstated in-band DTMF with the fixes the first attempt lacked, plus full live tunability:

  • Codec-aware: tones are encoded with the call's negotiated G.711 variant (connection.Codec), the same path as AI speech.
  • Realistic signal: standard dual-tone pairs with 2 dB forward twist (real handsets emit the high frequency ~2 dB louder; some detectors validate the ratio), 120 ms tones / 80 ms gaps — quick human keying.
  • Live tuning console: every knob is runtime-tweakable on stage. The provider reads the effective settings at every dial, so a change applies to the AI's very next key press — no redeploy, even mid-call between the IVR's retry prompts.
  • Per-dial config stamp (PR #133): each dial line in the transcript records the exact settings used — [AI dialed: 4059094# — in-band tone=120ms gap=80ms peak=0.35 twist=2dB lead=0ms] — so outcomes correlate with knob values without guesswork.
  • Audition before dialing: a Play button renders the exact waveform (same generator, same settings) in the browser.

5 · The experiment plan — things to try, in order

  1. In-band defaults (tone 120 / gap 80 / peak 0.35 / twist 2): 4–5 calls each to 4059094 and 5561466. The per-dial stamp in the transcript ties every attempt to its settings.
  2. Pacing sweep if misses persist: 100/100, 180/120, 250/250 — longer tones and gaps are easier to detect, at the cost of entry time.
  3. Leading silence 300–500 ms — rules out first-digit clipping while the line settles.
  4. Level sweep: peak 0.45 (louder), then twist 0 (equal bands) — some detectors are sensitive to absolute level or ratio.
  5. Cross-check send_dtmf: duration 400 with pause "" (back-to-back) vs w — now that pacing is tunable, the carrier path gets a fair sweep too.
  6. Portal variant: switch the Voice API application's DTMF type to Inband (codecs already G.711-only) and re-test send_dtmf — removes the RFC 2833 → PSTN conversion step.
  7. Blacktown regression check after any winning configuration — it must keep working.

If everything above still fails

  • Inbound-only media streaming for IVR calls (code change): today the app owns the outbound media leg (stream_bidirectional_mode: rtp), and send_dtmf injection has to race that stream. Streaming inbound-only hands the outbound leg back to Telnyx so carrier DTMF injection is conflict-free. Realtime calls keep bidirectional.
  • Telnyx Mission Control → Debugging: pull the SIP/RTP trace for one success and one failure and compare — was DTMF emitted, when, and as RFC 2833 vs in-band? This is wire-level ground truth.
  • Record and inspect: with in-band, the dialed tones travel the recorded media path — a failed call's recording can be spectrum-checked to see literally what Sydney Water heard.
  • Engage Telnyx support with call IDs: they can see the PSTN gateway leg we cannot.
