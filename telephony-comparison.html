📞 Telephony Architecture Report: InfoDialer AI Agent

Evaluating infrastructure as the routing layer for the Google gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview model (subject to discussion and review, but it's the best candidate so far).

🎯 Executive Summary

To build a conversational AI agent using the native multimodal gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview model (subject to discussion and review, but it's the best candidate so far), you need a "dumb pipe" to quickly bridge the Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) to Google's servers.

⏱️ Why Latency is the Ultimate Metric

In natural human conversation, response gaps are typically under 500 milliseconds (ms). If an AI takes longer than 800ms to respond, the "illusion of intelligence" immediately breaks. The human caller assumes the line is dead or the bot is broken, leading to them repeating themselves or talking over the AI (barge-in). Smooth turn-taking requires absolute minimal latency across the entire network chain so the AI can gracefully handle interruptions.

Based on first-principles analysis of network architecture, Telnyx remains the supreme choice (9/10). Because it operates a private Internet Protocol (IP) backbone and colocates servers directly at telecom Points of Presence (PoPs), it minimizes the physical distance audio must travel before reaching Google's AI. Azure Communication Services (ACS) is a strong secondary option due to its native Pulse-Code Modulation (PCM) audio support, which reduces transcoding compute time, though it inherently suffers from a cross-cloud network hop to reach Google.

✅ Telnyx Pros

  • Ultra-Low Transit: Private Multiprotocol Label Switching (MPLS) backbone bypasses public internet jitter (<200ms).
  • Physical Colocation: Servers sit directly inside telecom PoPs.
  • AU Compliance: Excellent portal for fast Australian Business Number (ABN) verification and high +61 number inventory.
  • Lowest Cost: At ~$0.02 / min for Australian outbound calls, it is 33% to 66% cheaper than Azure or AWS.

❌ Telnyx Cons

  • Transcoding Tax: Uses legacy G.711 μ-law audio, requiring +20-40ms to convert to the PCM format Gemini needs.
  • New Vendor Sprawl: InfoTrack currently utilizes AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud. Choosing Telnyx requires onboarding a net-new vendor outside your established cloud ecosystem.

🏗️ Strategic Context: Why Build Custom vs. Using Existing AI Call Providers?

Before evaluating telephony infrastructure, it is critical to justify why we are building the orchestration layer ourselves rather than using "off-the-shelf" AI voice platforms (e.g., Retell AI, Vapi, or Bland AI). The decision to build custom is driven by three primary first-principles factors:

1. True Multimodal Call Quality

Most existing providers rely on a cascaded architecture: Speech-to-Text (STT) → Text Large Language Model (LLM) → Text-to-Speech (TTS). This creates robotic conversational gaps and struggles with interruptions. Building custom allows us to utilize Gemini 3.1 Flash Live's native end-to-end voice architecture (Audio → LLM → Audio), resulting in vastly superior emotional intonation and instantaneous barge-in handling.

2. Cost Economics at Scale

Middleman providers charge a significant "orchestration tax" (often ~$0.05 to $0.15 per minute) on top of the raw LLM and telephony costs. By building the WebSocket bridging directly between the telephony provider (Telnyx/Azure) and Google, we operate at baseline infrastructure costs, radically improving margins at scale.

3. Granular Customization & Tool Calling

Pre-built platforms lock you into their specific Application Programming Interface (API) schemas and predefined logic flows. A custom build gives us absolute, low-level control over the real-time audio buffers and asynchronous function calling, making deep integration with InfoTrack's internal databases much more robust.

⚡ First Principles Reasoning: The Latency Equation

🧠 1. AI Inference (The Brain)

The gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview model consumes the majority of the delay (~70%). It must ingest the audio, process the Large Language Model (LLM) context, and generate native audio back.

🌩️ 2. Network Transit (Cross-Cloud)

Audio traveling from the telephony server to Google Cloud. Using providers that rely on the public internet (Twilio) creates unpredictable jitter here.

📱 3. PSTN / Cellular Hop

The physical radio waves and fiber optics routing the call from an Australian mobile phone to the telecom data center in Sydney.

🔄 4. Transcoding Overhead

Converting traditional G.711 μ-law telecom audio into the PCM format required by Gemini. Azure ACS avoids this by natively streaming PCM.
