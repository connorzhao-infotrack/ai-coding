SETTLEIT-10754 · next-step engine · build decision

How smart should the suggestion engine be?

One question: how much of the next step do we let the model decide?

The proactive-agent behaviour (auto-advance + suggestion pills) is really one engine with a dial on it — how much autonomy the LLM gets. These aren't three unrelated designs; they're three points on that dial. Here's each one as a flow, rated honestly, with a recommendation that walks the dial rather than planting a flag on it.

What we're actually building

Two behaviours: the agent just does it, or it offers you pills

The shapes are already live in the interaction lab — this whole page is about how to decide and produce them. When there's one obvious next step the agent auto-advances (no click); at a crossroad it docks 1–4 suggestion pills above the input.

1 and 2a tie at 22 — they optimise opposite things (certainty vs. intelligence). The right move isn't to break the tie; it's to have both paths in one engine (rightmost column): deterministic by default, LLM-ranked only at the crossroads. That combination dominates every column it needs to.

Recommendation

Don't pick a point on the dial — walk it

Ship deterministic, add ranking where it's fuzzy, hold generation in reserve

Because Options 1 and 2a share the same catalogue and the same signal contract, they aren't a fork — they're phases of one build. Start fully deterministic (it already covers most of Lexi's chart), then switch on the LLM only at the genuine crossroads without touching the contract.

Phase 1 · Option 1
Catalogue + transition map + signal. Deterministic only. Covers auto-advance & known branches.
→
Phase 2 · Option 2a
LLM ranks the catalogue at fuzzy crossroads (financials). Same contract.
→
Hold · Option 2b
Only if the catalogue proves too rigid. Its guardrail cost isn't justified while the catalogue covers the real actions.

Why not jump to 2b: the guardrail that makes generated suggestions safe is a single point of failure and the hardest thing here to test — and "figures a client relies on, offered by a model-invented step" is exactly the kind of sentence that's hard to defend to Christian Beck. Keep generation in reserve; the catalogue is doing the work.

The one decision for the team: is the transition map code (guards touch order state) with a data-driven label layer on top (PM/Lexi tune copy without a deploy)? That's the split RenderToolCatalogue already uses — reusing it keeps this consistent with what's built.
