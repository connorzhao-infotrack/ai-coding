⚖️ The trade-off (read this first)

The three principles

  1. Higher order volume = better. Onboarding effort is roughly fixed per council/authority, so we want maximum orders per integration.
  2. Easy phone UX for AI = better. Deterministic IVR or pre-recorded menus > live human handoff. Live humans with diverse accents > nothing, but require natural conversation, identity verification, and tolerate redirects to web forms.
  3. Authority sharing = better. Every additional water authority we plug in is a new phone number, new IVR, new identity-verification flow, new test rig. Reuse beats one-offs.

The QLD-vs-NSW tension

  • QLD wins on raw volume — 52 orders (62%) is in 3 councils.
  • NSW wins on authority reuse — all 4 NSW councils share Sydney Water (already locked in / done). Adding the 4 NSW councils is a council-only integration; the water side is free.
  • QLD costs 3 new water authorities — QUU (Brisbane), Unitywater (Moreton Bay), Gold Coast Water (which is actually a council business unit, so it's one combined integration for the Gold Coast).
  • Brisbane alone (29 orders) ≈ all 4 NSW councils combined (32 orders) — and Brisbane has the hardest phone UX (no IVR, push to online form).
Headline read: NSW is the cheap, fast win — 4 councils, 1 already-built authority, 32 orders. QLD is where the real volume lives but every QLD council brings a new water authority and Brisbane is genuinely hard to automate over the phone. Recommendation below: ship NSW first, then add Moreton Bay (not Brisbane) as the first QLD pilot because Unitywater has a documented 24/7 self-serve IVR — the only one in this set.

🟢 QLD deep-dive (the volume bet)
QLD context: rates and water are split between council and SEQ water retailer, so each property in QLD typically requires two data sources (council rates + water authority). NSW (excluding the Hunter and Central Coast) typically combines water into the council rates certificate. This is why "QLD orders" sound smaller per integration than they really are: each Brisbane order is council + QUU; each Moreton Bay order is council + Unitywater.

🏙️ Brisbane City Council — 29 orders · AI ease 2 / 5

Phone numbers

07 3403 8888 — Main Contact Centre · 24/7 · goes straight to a human, no IVR brisbane.qld.gov.au/about-council/contact-council
133 263 (133 BNE) — Business Hotline · 24/7 · business services only (not residential rates) business-hotline-133-bne

13 26 57 — Urban Utilities (water) — separate entity, see QUU section rates-and-water-balance-enquiries

IVR / automation

No IVR on the main contact centre. Per Verint's interview with Shane Hackett (Manager Customer Services, BCC): "Our agents, we have no IVR, so every time they pick up a phone call, answer an email, respond to a social media post, or have a person come in to the counter, it could be on one of those 4,431 enquiry types." Westender confirms BCC "stands out as one of the few contact centres nationally that ensures callers always get to speak to someone." (verint.com, westender.com.au)

Council policy steers rates-balance enquiries to the online form, not phone. The "Check rates balance" page directs users to an online form (free, immediate). Conveyancer-grade balances require a paid "inspection of records search" with documented identity (services.brisbane.qld.gov.au/online-services/conveyancing/conveyance-search-rates).

Wait times

~180 agents across Chermside and Yeronga centres. Target: answer within 60 seconds; reported ~90% answered within 20s. ~3,300 enquiries/day, ~735,000 phone calls/year (grahamquirk.com.au).

Why score 2 / 5

  • No IVR + no automated balance line → AI must conduct natural Australian-English conversation with a live human.
  • Council policy actively steers rates-balance enquiries to the online form — agents may refuse and redirect.
  • Conveyancer-grade balances require paid online search with verified identity. Not phone-friendly.
  • The one automated path (1300 309 311) is a payments-only IVR.
  • Saving grace: short wait (~20s), 24/7, service-oriented. So it's a 2 (hard, but not impossible) — not a 1.

Volume vs. effort

29 orders is the biggest single prize in the set. But the BCC integration is the hardest of any QLD council to automate over the phone, and the agent will almost certainly redirect the AI to brisbane.qld.gov.au/online-services/rates-services/rates-balance-enquiry. The realistic path here is either (a) web-scrape the online form rather than dial, or (b) operate the AI as a fully-believable ratepayer conversation with account number + property address pre-loaded.

🏖️ Moreton Bay Regional Council — 14 orders · AI ease 3 / 5

Phone numbers

07 3205 0555 — Main council contact (rates, general, after-hours emergencies all route here) moretonbay.qld.gov.au/Contact

IVR / automation

Single advertised number for everything — IVR menu structure is not published. Whether there's a "press 1 for rates" tree behind the line or it goes straight to a queue is undocumented. Self-service is pushed to the Moreton Bay Online Services / eServices portal (eservices.moretonbay.qld.gov.au) for balance + payment history. BPAY is the bank-side automated path. No dedicated automated rates-balance phone line.

Accent / dialect note

South-East Queensland operators, predominantly general Australian English. Local place names a caller will encounter — Caboolture (Aboriginal "Kabul-tur", Place of Carpet Snakes), Burpengary, Narangba, Redcliffe, Strathpine, Bribie Island — are the main ASR risk. Indigenous-origin names most likely to trip Whisper. Build in a confirmation/read-back loop on any address-related field.

Why score 3 / 5

  • + Single advertised number, standard hours, English-speaking operators, council pushes most self-service to web (so phone queues are focused).
  • − No documented automated rates-balance IVR → AI must reach a human and verbally authenticate.
  • − Indigenous place names (Caboolture, Burpengary, Narangba) add ASR/TTS risk.
  • − No published IVR map — AI must discover menu live (if one exists).

The big asset: Unitywater has the only documented self-serve IVR in this set

See QUU vs Unitywater vs Gold Coast Water below — Unitywater (Moreton Bay's water authority) is the only SEQ water authority with a published 24/7 "press 2 for balance" IVR. That makes Moreton Bay the most AI-friendly QLD pick for the water half of the workflow.

Risk: low. Worst case: 1 council slips, we still ship 3.

📡 Landscape notes (IVR + accent + tech stack)

IVR commonality across Australian councils

Per ACXPA (Australian Customer Experience Professionals Association) mystery-shopping research, councils have the simplest IVR experiences of any sector benchmarked: average ~1.0 menu options vs national average 1.9, average depth 0.6 layers (banks average 3.2). 36% of councils have no IVR at all — calls go straight to a human queue. Only one council in their study (City of Sydney) had two layers of IVR. (ACXPA Q4 2023, Q4 2024)

Implication: our AI must be tuned for natural-language conversation with humans, not DTMF traversal. Water authorities (Sydney Water, QUU, Unitywater) tend to have slightly deeper IVRs because they segment developer/customer/faults — Unitywater's 24/7 self-serve "press 2" is the standout.

Accent / ASR risk

Peer-reviewed evaluation of OpenAI Whisper (JASA Express Letters) and independent benchmarks (Mozilla Data Collective, HuggingFace) consistently find British and Australian English produce ~30% higher word error rate than American English. The newer gpt-realtime model is claimed to handle accents better, but OpenAI's own docs explicitly tell developers to test against target accents. Main failure modes: AU vowel shifts (FACE/PRICE/MOUTH diphthongs), high-rising terminal intonation, rapid street/suburb name speech. Recommendation: neutral or mild AU TTS voice, post-process for AU place names + currency, build read-back confirmation loops on numerics, DTMF fallback when recogniser fails twice. (JASA, Mozilla DC, HuggingFace, OpenAI gpt-realtime)

Tech stack — Genesys Cloud is dominant

Genesys Cloud underpins NSW's whole-of-government Virtual Contact Centre (GTP VCC) via Digital NSW, is resold by Telstra Enterprise as "Telstra Contact Centre", and has named local-government case studies. Avaya is being migrated off. Practical implications for our AI: expect Genesys-style "this call may be recorded" prompts, standard queue music, and ANI/CLI-driven agent screen-pops — our AI's caller-ID will likely be associated with a CRM record after the first call. Most councils ride hosted/cloud telephony, so G.711/Opus audio quality should be acceptable. (Digital NSW VCC, CX Directory)
